RoadBook — Privacy Policy

Effective 25 October 2026.

RoadBook records car journeys and turns them into a mileage log and route posters. It runs entirely on your iPhone.

The short version. There is no account, no server and no analytics. Nothing you record is sent to us, because there is nowhere for it to go — we operate no service that could receive it. The only things that leave your phone are described below, and they go to Apple or to wherever you choose to send a file yourself.

What RoadBook stores, and where

Everything is kept in the app's own storage on your device. It is included in your device backups if you have those switched on, and those backups are yours — they are not visible to us.

Journeys. The route of each drive as a series of positions with timestamps and speeds; the start and end time; distance, driving time and top speed; the names of the places you started and finished; and any notes you add.

How you classify them. Which trip type a drive is filed under, and the types themselves — the three that ship, plus any you add.

What a drive was for. The purpose you write on a trip, in your own words. This is free text, so how identifying it is depends entirely on what you type — "client visit" and "Dr Kelly, 9am" are both valid and only one of them is anonymous. It is stored on this device with everything else, and it appears in the CSV mileage report you export. The same is true of a trip type you create and of any notes you add.

Vehicles. The names you give your cars, and their number plates if you enter them. A number plate is optional. It is never shown alongside your trips, never added to a poster and never included in a GPX export — it appears only on a mileage report you choose to export, which is the reason it is asked for.

Your name. Optional. It is used for one thing: printing on a mileage report you export, so the document says whose driving it records. It is never shown alongside your trips, never added to a poster and never included in a GPX export.

Posters you keep. The finished image, its title, and a reference to the drive it was made from.

Reports you send. Each mileage report you share, PDF or CSV, is kept exactly as it was sent, so you can see later what you handed in. It holds what the report itself holds — the trips, their places and purposes, and your name, vehicle and number plate if you entered them — along with when it was sent. Keeping it sends nothing anywhere: the copy stays on this device. You can delete any of them from the Reports tab.

Your settings. Units, appearance, the car colour and size, whether automatic recording is on, the poster route-trim switches, and which period the mileage log last showed.

What leaves your device

Three things, all of them to Apple, and nothing to us.

Place names. When a drive finishes, RoadBook sends its start and end coordinates to Apple's geocoding service to turn them into a place name, so your trip reads "Aarhus → Aalborg" rather than a pair of numbers. Two points per drive. The route itself is never sent.

Map tiles. The maps shown while driving and when viewing a trip request map tiles from Apple for the area on screen, as any map does. Three poster styles — Atlas, Nocturne and Satellite — request tiles for the area you drove, which tells Apple roughly where the drive was. Every other poster style is drawn from the recorded track alone and requests nothing. A video is always made in one of those three styles, so making one always requests tiles; a video whose frame follows the car requests the same area of map in more detail, and never a wider one.

Purchases. RoadBook Pro is sold as a yearly subscription — which can begin with a free week — or as a one-off lifetime purchase, all handled entirely by Apple. The free week is Apple's introductory offer, so whether you have already had one is Apple's record, not ours. We never see your payment details, and RoadBook holds no billing information of any kind. To know what you have paid for, the app asks Apple's purchase records on your device — including whether you ever subscribed, which is what keeps the mileage log and its reports open after a subscription ends. Nothing about this reaches us.

Apple's handling of the above is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Files you send yourself

RoadBook can export GPX files, posters, videos, and a mileage report as a CSV or a PDF. These are created on your device and handed to the iOS share sheet, or — when you save a poster or video to Photos or to Files — to the system's own save panel. Where they go — email, Files, a messaging app, a colleague — is entirely your choice, and RoadBook plays no part after you choose. A GPX file contains the full route of a drive, and a bulk export contains the full route of every drive you selected. A CSV mileage report contains, for every trip in it: the dates and times it started and ended, the places, the distance and driving time, the trip type and its purpose, and — if you have entered them — your name, the vehicle and its number plate. A PDF report carries the totals and then every trip in it — its date and times, places, purpose, type and distance — with your name, vehicle and number plate.

A poster or video carries no location metadata. It is not derived from a photograph but drawn from scratch at export, so there is no EXIF on it at all — not even where a photograph of your own is used as the backdrop. What a poster does show is the shape of the route, which is why the route-trim switches exist: they cut a random amount off each end of the drawn line so a poster does not publish the address you set off from or arrived at. Your figures still cover the whole drive, so the line and the numbers deliberately disagree.

Permissions

Location. Required to record a drive at all.

While Using the App is enough to record drives you start yourself, including while the app is in the background or your phone is locked — iOS shows a blue indicator for as long as this is happening.

Always additionally allows automatic recording. With it enabled, RoadBook watches for you leaving places you usually park and for the movement that signals a drive beginning, and iOS may start the app in the background to do so, including after your phone restarts. iOS also sometimes starts it when you have not moved at all, and RoadBook then uses GPS for a few minutes to check whether a drive has begun. This is why you may sometimes see the location indicator when you are not driving. Turning automatic recording off in Settings stops it.

Motion & Fitness. Optional. Used to tell driving apart from walking, which makes automatic recording more accurate and lets it start a drive sooner. This data is read on the device and is not stored beyond the start time it helps establish.

Photo library — adding only. Requested the first time you save a poster or video to your photos, and only for adding to it. RoadBook never gains access to read your photo library. Choosing a photograph as a poster background uses the system photo picker, which runs outside the app and hands over only the single image you select.

What we collect

Nothing.

RoadBook contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking of any kind, and no third-party SDKs or frameworks. No data is transmitted to the developer, and we have no server, database or account system that could hold it. We therefore hold no personal data about you, and there is nothing for you to request access to, correct, or ask us to delete.

Erasing your data

Settings → Erase All Driving Data removes, in one action: every trip and its route, every poster you have kept, every report you have sent, your vehicles and their number plates, your name, and every trip purpose you have written. Trip types go back to the three the app ships with. Your preferences — units, appearance, the car, the poster switches — are kept, because they record nothing about where you have been.

The places RoadBook watches for the start of a drive go with it. Those are worked out from where your past drives ended, so once the drives are gone there is nothing left to watch and the watching stops.

Nothing is recoverable afterwards. There is no copy on a server, because there is no server. If you want one, export your trips as GPX first — the trip list's selection menu will write every drive you select into a single file.

Deleting the app

Deleting RoadBook removes everything described above from your device — your journeys, trip types and purposes, vehicles, your name, posters, sent reports and settings. Nothing is left behind. Your purchases stay with your Apple Account, and are restored from there if you install RoadBook again.

Children

RoadBook is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.
